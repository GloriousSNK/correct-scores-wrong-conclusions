\begin{abstract}
Learned forecasters increasingly stand in for scientific models that are themselves imperfect,
and a forecast score alone cannot say whether a surrogate succeeds for the right reasons. We
test five conditions that connect a score to a scientific claim (held-out generalization,
complete failure accounting, matched disclosure, structural identifiability, and cross-system
transfer) on $k$-pendulum ($k\in\{1,2,3\}$) and Lorenz--63 forecasts from
language models, a time-series foundation model, learned dynamics, sparse identification, and
numerical integrators. Every test alters or overturns a conclusion the raw comparison invites: the
in-sample/out-of-sample gap runs up to elevenfold, two exact symmetries make absolute parameter
recovery impossible, and a second system reverses the model ordering. On video-tracked
hardware the roles invert: the RK4 model with published constants is itself imperfect, and
its lead over simple baselines is gone within a second. A correct leaderboard can still
support incorrect scientific conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Scientific claims require more than low forecast error. When a learned forecaster stands in
for an imperfect scientific model, a result may depend on training trajectories, dropped
failures, unmatched inputs, an unidentifiable target, or one favorable system: failures of the
evaluation rather than of the surrogate. We study these failure modes directly,
predicting a chaotic system's state $\mathbf{s}_T$ from its state $\mathbf{s}_0$ on two
systems chosen to disagree: the $k$-link pendulum, chaotic for $k{\ge}2$, with high-precision
ground truth and control over what physical metadata a model is told, and Lorenz--63, which
differs in observation model and hypothesis class; a hardware replication of the audit
(\S\ref{sec:realdata}) then scores the reference model itself as the imperfect one.

Our contribution is a claim audit for dynamical-system forecasts: held-out generalization,
complete failure accounting, matched disclosure on identical trajectories, identifiability
checked before scoring recovery, and transfer to a second system. The five are indexed to
the claims, not drawn from a taxonomy: each tests one inference step between a score and a
conclusion (sample to population, answered cells to full task, correlation to attribution,
accuracy to parameter recovery, one system to a family claim), and claims we do not make
would need other checks, such as calibration for probabilistic forecasts.
Table~\ref{tab:audit-summary} states the conclusion before and after each test; all five
alter or reject a conclusion the unaudited comparison supports, and these are conclusions
the field draws: zero-shot forecasters are routinely scored without failure accounting or
matched controls \citep{gruver2023llmtime,zhang2025zeroshotchaos}. Three checks are
the familiar concerns of imperfect models applied to the evaluation: misspecification
(a Hamiltonian prior on damped dynamics), identifiability, and transfer.

\section{Benchmark design}
\subsection{Task and systems}
The pendulum state is $\mathbf{s}_0=(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_k,\omega_1,\dots,\omega_k)\in
\mathbb{R}^{2k}$; hiding its constants does not change the trajectory, which permits the
matched test in \S\ref{sec:sysid}. Ground truth integrates the full nonlinear
Lagrangian equations of motion with fixed-step RK4 at $\Delta t{=}10^{-3}$\,s, recorded every $0.01$\,s plus a
$10$\,s pre-$t{=}0$ context for the time-series models. Initial
conditions are sampled as $\theta_i\sim\mathcal{U}(-3,3)$\,rad,
$\omega_i\sim\mathcal{U}(-1,1)$\,rad/s with a fixed seed; the labels come from RK4, so its
coarser-step variant is a reference by construction, not a competitor.

\subsection{Regimes and grid}
Three regimes test generalization: \texttt{normal} ($g{=}9.81$, undamped),
\texttt{changed\_disclosed} ($g{=}3.71$, damped), and \texttt{changed\_hidden} ($g{=}1.62$,
damped, constants withheld; full values in Table~\ref{tab:regimes}). A cell is a
tuple (model, $k$, regime, horizon) with horizons
$\{0.01,1,10,60\}$\,s. The LLM grid also varies modality and prompting; all comparisons here
use the coordinate modality with direct prompting.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample protocol.} Learned models are never scored on their training data: a
shared held-out set (a separate seed) of $180$ initial conditions ($60$ per regime,
$k\in\{1,2,3\}$) is scored identically for every family. The cross-family comparison uses
horizons $\{1,10\}$\,s, where chaos has begun but error has not saturated
($180\times2=360$ cells per model); in-sample error appears once, to quantify the
generalization gap (\S\ref{sec:reliability}).

\subsection{Inputs and grouping}
\label{sec:inputs}
Each family sees different information; the asymmetry is part of the experimental record
(Table~\ref{tab:inputs}, Appendix~B). The integrators get the exact equations and constants;
the learned models get target-system training plus the constants (normal-regime priors in
hidden cells); the LLMs get a text description with the constants except in hidden cells;
Chronos-2 gets a $10$\,s pre-$t{=}0$ history window and no constants at all. The LLMs are large
hosted models, while the learned models are small MLPs (Appendix~B); larger
physics-informed networks are out of scope. We separate known-physics methods (RK4, Euler,
symplectic), which serve as numerical references, from the black-box methods being compared.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
The primary metric is mean absolute angle error (rad), wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi]$ and averaged
over links and trajectories; angles alone are scored, since wrapping gives answers and
failures one bounded scale that unbounded velocities lack. A cell is answered only if the
model returns a parseable, finite prediction; because scoring answered cells alone would
reward abstention on hard cells, unanswered cells receive the random-guess baseline $\pi/2$
(the expected wrapped-angle error of a uniform guess). Answering is part of the score: the
task is fixed-coverage forecasting, not selective prediction.

\paragraph{Verification record.} Each cell retains its identifiers, prompt, raw response,
parser outcome, prediction, token counts, and score. Local methods are reproducible from the
released checkpoints and seeds; hosted deployments can change or be withdrawn, so for them
the released per-cell responses are the record that keeps the evaluation auditable.

\paragraph{Disclosure gap.} We define
$\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}=\mathrm{err}(\text{hidden})-\mathrm{err}(\text{disclosed})$; positive
values indicate a penalty for withholding constants. Because regime contrasts confound
disclosure with trajectory difficulty, \S\ref{sec:sysid} also toggles disclosure on identical
trajectories.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{LLMs.} We evaluate \texttt{kimi-k2.6}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}, and
\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} through an OpenAI-compatible API (hosted deployments, June~2026),
with a structured prompt and JSON schema (Appendix~A); each cell is one temperature-$0$ call
with uniform budgets, no best-of-$N$.

\paragraph{Time-series foundation models.} We run Chronos-2 \citep{ansari2024chronos}
locally in its standard history-conditioned form: a $10$\,s pre-$t{=}0$ context of all $2k$
channels forecasts to the target horizon, in univariate and multivariate modes (the paired
mode comparison uses $20$ trajectories per cell).

\paragraph{Learned dynamics.} Neural~ODE, HNN, and LNN share a $256$-wide, $3$-layer tanh
MLP and integrate at inference with \texttt{scipy}; we compare derivative-matching, rollout, and
mixed-window rollout objectives (Appendix~B). The primary models train on nine trajectories,
one per $(k,\mathrm{regime})$ cell; a nested budget study trains fresh checkpoints at $\{1,5,20\}$
trajectories per eligible cell with three initializations (HNN: undamped cells only, since
damping violates Hamiltonian conservation).

\paragraph{Integrators.} Euler, RK4, and St\"ormer--Verlet (symplectic) at $\Delta
t{=}0.01$\,s using the exact equations of motion; their error is from discretization alone.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Four baselines share the grid: persistence
($\theta_T{=}\theta_0$), constant velocity ($\theta_T{=}\theta_0{+}\omega_0T$), a
small-angle linearized model, and a nearest-neighbor analog forecast built from the learned
models' training data. On both held-out seeds (equal-weighted; \S\ref{sec:replication}) we
also fit SINDy (degree-two, STLSQ) and NVAR (two delays, degree-two, ridge); divergent
rollouts are failures at $\pi/2$.

\paragraph{Lorenz replication.} A secondary Lorenz--63 test compares numerical references,
persistence, SINDy, NVAR, a Neural~ODE trained on $20$ single-seed trajectories, and zero-shot
Chronos-2 on five five-trajectory held-out seeds, reporting training-normalized coordinate MAE
at $\{0.1,0.5,1.0\}$\,s (Appendix~C). HNN and LNN are omitted:
Lorenz--63 is dissipative and non-Hamiltonian.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Failure accounting and held-out generalization}
\label{sec:reliability}
Every model is scored on the shared held-out grid of $360$ cells ($180$ trajectories, horizons
$\{1,10\}$\,s); the Answered column of Table~\ref{tab:overall} gives the fraction with valid
predictions. \texttt{grok} answers $82\%$ and \texttt{lnn} $94\%$: their ranks fall when
missing cells receive $\pi/2$, and a mean over answered cells alone would flatter both.
Under the strict $\pi$ bound only these two move (\texttt{grok} eight places down, the LNN
still last), so the ordering is not an artifact of the imputation constant.
We report $95\%$ bootstrap intervals over trajectories (LLM per-call variation excluded).

Table~\ref{tab:overall} ranks the models under the access conditions of \S\ref{sec:inputs};
the ranking is conditional on those inputs, this training budget, and this system
(\S\ref{sec:lorenz} tests transfer). The numerical integrators sit at the top;
every learned-dynamics model lands closer to the $\pi/2$ baseline than to the
RK4 and symplectic references. The lowest-error black-box entry, the LLM \texttt{kimi} ($0.538$\,rad, $99\%$
answered), has an interval that only marginally excludes the nearest baseline's: a statement about
one recorded June~2026 deployment, not about model families; the Lorenz test rejects the
family reading.

Under the equalized hidden-regime run, nearest-neighbor lookup ($0.725$) and the
linearized pendulum ($0.855$) beat every learned model, and persistence ($1.004$) beats all
but HNN-rollout ($0.972$), though those two intervals overlap. The stable learned models
score $0.09$--$0.63$\,rad on their training trajectories against $0.97$--$1.14$ held out
(Table~\ref{tab:insample}), a $1.7$--$11\times$ gap widest for the in-sample winner,
the mixed-window Neural~ODE.

SINDy ($0.883$\,rad) and NVAR ($1.248$) appear only on the two-seed replication subset
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}); SINDy lands between Chronos-2 and the learned models, NVAR near
constant velocity.

\subsection{Matched disclosure and identifiability}
\label{sec:sysid}
On the held-out set the hidden regime shows lower error than the disclosed ($-0.390$\,rad,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{<}10^{-3}$, unpaired) and normal ($-0.524$) regimes; but the regimes use
different trajectories, and the hidden ones are slower and less divergent. Toggling
disclosure on identical trajectories for the three LLMs ($900$ paired cells on a separate
matched grid; Table~\ref{tab:sysid}) reverses the pooled effect: hiding the constants
increases error ($\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}{=}{+}0.069$\,rad, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{=}0.034$),
with a strong penalty at $k{=}1$ and a small reverse effect at $k{=}3$; the unadjusted
advantage is a difficulty
confound.

The disclosure test also has a structural limit: the angular equations are unchanged by
$(m,d)\mapsto(am,ad)$ and $(L,g,d)\mapsto(bL,bg,b^2d)$, so for $k{=}1$ only $g/L$ and
$d/(mL^2)$ are identifiable, and absolute $g$, $L$, $m$, $d$ cannot
be scored as independently recoverable. A single-state constant-naming probe mostly returns
Earth-standard defaults (a prior, not recovery; transcripts released); a numerical check verifies both invariances
to below $10^{-13}$.

\subsection{Replication across seeds and training budgets}
\label{sec:replication}
On an independent held-out seed (five trajectories per cell versus $20$;
Table~\ref{tab:replication}), the selected ordering is unchanged: numerical references
lowest, Chronos-2 ahead of the learned models, HNN-rollout best among them. Two seeds do not
estimate seed-level uncertainty. The LNN's low primary-seed coverage was a hardware
artifact: its per-step Hessian exhausted GPU memory on long rollouts. A CPU rerun recovers the
dropped cells ($66\%\to94\%$; both seeds use it); the remaining failures are genuine
divergences, scored at $\pi/2$, and the two-seed mean stays $1.366$\,rad.

\paragraph{Training-budget check.}
Across three initializations and both held-out seeds, Neural~ODE error falls from $1.090$ at
one trajectory per cell to $0.861$ at twenty, and normal-regime HNN error from $1.269$ to
$0.981$ (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). The rows cover different regimes; more data helps the
Neural~ODE monotonically, the HNN plateaus by $15$ trajectories, and neither closes the gap
to known physics.

\subsection{Cross-system test: Lorenz--63}
\label{sec:lorenz}
We test whether the pendulum conclusions transfer to Lorenz--63, a chaotic system with a
polynomial vector field. They do not. The identifiability verdict flips: on the pendulum the
physical constants are not recoverable from an angle trajectory (\S\ref{sec:sysid}), while on
Lorenz, SINDy recovers the governing equations themselves, not merely accurate forecasts. The
refit selects exactly the true sparsity pattern and returns
$(\hat\sigma,\hat\rho,\hat\beta)=(10,28,8/3)$ with maximum coefficient error $2.2\times10^{-13}$
(Appendix~C); forecast error stays below $2\times10^{-5}$ in every seed. The difference is
the setting, not the audit: Lorenz--63 lies inside SINDy's hypothesis class and is fully
observed; the pendulum's absolute constants are unidentifiable from angles under any method.
Identifiability is a property of the observation model and the hypothesis class, and the
check makes it explicit before recovery is scored.

The model ordering also changes. Trained on one seed and evaluated on five held-out seeds
($25$ trajectories), a Lorenz Neural~ODE reaches normalized MAE $0.014\pm0.008$, $0.079\pm0.030$,
and $0.277\pm0.063$ at $0.1$, $0.5$, and $1.0$\,s (mean$\,\pm\,$SE over seeds), below both
Chronos-2 modes at every horizon (multivariate $0.101\pm0.018$,
$0.691\pm0.129$, $0.771\pm0.097$; univariate similar); NVAR drifts to $0.429$ by $1.0$\,s and
persistence trails ($0.900$; Table~\ref{tab:replication}). This reverses the pendulum result, where trained models lose to
simple baselines: here the system-trained model beats the zero-shot foundation model. The
families again see different inputs, so this is not an architecture race; what the
cross-system result rejects is the pendulum ranking as a general ordering of model families.

The reversal does not rest on a favorable seed: the Neural~ODE beats both Chronos-2 modes in
all $15$ seed-by-horizon comparisons, and the identifiability result is not a sampling
claim, since the symmetries are exact and Lorenz--63 lies in SINDy's library by construction.
The test excludes the API LLMs, HNN, and LNN (a Hamiltonian surrogate is misspecified for the
dissipative flow); those omissions limit what it can establish, not what it can refute, and
the Lorenz ordering is not offered as the correct one.

\subsection{Real data: the reference model becomes the imperfect one}
\label{sec:realdata}
On real hardware RK4 is no longer ground truth but the imperfect model. We use an open
video-tracked double pendulum \citep{myers2020pendulum} (three free-drop trials,
$500$\,Hz, published point-mass constants, ${\sim}0.35^{\circ}$ median tracking noise);
Trial~1 serves only for calibration (nearest-neighbor database, SINDy fit, damping fit),
and Trials 2--3 supply $120$ held-out states scored as before at $\{0.1,0.5,1.0\}$\,s
(Table~\ref{tab:realdata}, Appendix~\ref{app:realdata}).

RK4 with the published constants leads at $0.1$\,s ($0.084$\,rad, about $14\times$ the
tracking noise) but reaches $1.25$\,rad by $1.0$\,s, indistinguishable from persistence
($1.23$; paired difference $+0.02$, CI $[-0.12,+0.16]$); unmodeled friction and the
point-mass simplification consume its advantage within a second. The fitted damping trades
horizons ($0.89$ vs.\ $0.82$ at $0.5$\,s, $1.15$ vs.\ $1.25$ at $1.0$\,s; the $1.0$\,s gain
is significant, CI $[-0.20,-0.00]$). SINDy, exact on Lorenz, sits at baseline
level here ($1.54$); the hardware's dynamics are not in its library. Chronos-2 answers $89\%$
of cells: states shortly after the drop lack its $5$\,s context
(Appendix~\ref{app:realdata}), and failure accounting scores them at $\pi/2$. Every check
transfers, and the ordering differs from both synthetic orderings.

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:behavior}
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} reports the rest; two matter most: the
physical-fidelity check localizes a misspecification (a symplectic HNN rollout leaves energy drift
unchanged: the fault is the learned Hamiltonian, not the integrator), and
every black-box family saturates near the chaotic noise floor by $1$\,s, hence the
$\{1,10\}$\,s window. Error rises with $k$ for all but one model; the hidden regime is easier
for most models (the \S\ref{sec:sysid} confound); rollout loss is not uniformly better; and
Chronos-2's two modes are indistinguishable ($n{=}720$).

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
Performance depends on system, observations, hypothesis class, data, and access, which a
scalar leaderboard does not encode: each of the five checks changes a claim it supports, so
benchmarks should report checks, training budgets, and information access with
the result. The synthetic systems make each check's effect
visible against exact ground truth; the hardware check runs the audit where the reference
model itself is imperfect; all records to repeat it accompany the paper.
\textbf{Limitations.}\label{sec:limitations} The LLM grid uses one held-out seed; the Lorenz
test has five trajectories per seed, the hardware check three trials of one apparatus, SINDy
needs the true degree in its library, and foundation-model corpora may contain pendulum-like
data.

\section{Prompt templates}
The prompts used for every LLM evaluation cell are reproduced below verbatim.

\paragraph{System prompt (direct / no-CoT).}
\begin{small}\begin{verbatim}
You are a careful physicist predicting the future state of a k-pendulum (a chain of
k point masses on massless rods, hinged in a planar chain, swinging under gravity
with viscous damping). Respond with ONLY a single JSON object on one line, no prose,
no code fences. Schema:
{"theta": [..k floats..], "omega": [..k floats..]}
theta_i is in radians (link i measured from straight-down,
positive counter-clockwise)
and omega_i is angular velocity in rad/s.
\end{verbatim}\end{small}

\paragraph{System prompt (chain-of-thought).} Same preamble, then: ``Reason step-by-step
about the dynamics, then output your final answer. Wrap the final answer in
\texttt{<answer>...</answer>} tags containing ONLY a single JSON object:
\{"theta": [..k floats..], "omega": [..k floats..]\}.''

\paragraph{User prompt (coordinate modality).}
\begin{small}\begin{verbatim}
Number of links k = {k}
Physical constants:
  g (gravity, m/s^2): {g}
  L (rod lengths, m): {L}
  m (point masses, kg): {m}
  damping (viscous coefficient on omega): {damping}
Initial state at t=0:
  theta_0 (rad): {theta0}
  omega_0 (rad/s): {omega0}
Predict the state at t = {T} seconds.
Return theta and omega as length-{k} JSON arrays.
\end{verbatim}\end{small}

\paragraph{Hidden-constants regime.} The constants block above is replaced by:
\begin{small}\begin{verbatim}
Physical constants: HIDDEN. The numerical values of gravity, rod lengths, point
masses, and damping are NOT disclosed. You must infer dynamics from the initial
conditions alone.
\end{verbatim}\end{small}

\paragraph{Parsing and scoring.} Each response is stripped of code fences and (for CoT) of
everything outside the \texttt{<answer>...</answer>} tags; the first \texttt{\{...\}} object
is JSON-parsed and validated to contain length-$k$ \texttt{theta} and \texttt{omega} arrays.
A response that fails any check is counted as unanswered and scored at $\pi/2$.

\paragraph{Prompting, modality, and repeatability axes.}
\label{app:axes}
The released grid also varies prompting and input modality; from the saved records
(trajectory-level paired bootstrap throughout): (i)~Chain-of-thought does not change the
model ordering, and for the best-scoring LLM it is significantly \emph{worse} under
reliability-adjusted scoring ($0.659$ vs.\ $0.538$\,rad; paired difference $+0.121$, CI
$[+0.061,+0.183]$): reasoning consumes the token budget, unanswered cells rise from $6$ to
$50$ of $360$, and failure accounting---not reasoning quality---drives the gap. For the
other two models CoT is indistinguishable ($-0.038$, CI $[-0.102,+0.025]$; $-0.041$, CI
$[-0.107,+0.021]$). (ii)~On the one-trajectory-per-cell modality grid, replacing
coordinates with a rendered image never helps (significantly worse for one model, $+0.210$,
CI $[+0.033,+0.392]$), adding an image to coordinates changes nothing measurable, and the
model without vision support fails every image cell, which fixed-coverage scoring records
at $\pi/2$: modality is an access variable, not a free axis. (iii)~A second independent
temperature-$0$ run of one model's $216$-cell grid agrees bit-identically on $172/216$
cells but shifts the aggregate by $+0.073$\,rad (CI $[+0.031,+0.120]$), so hosted
temperature-$0$ inference is not exactly repeatable; this measured drift is why hosted
results are reproducible only from the saved records.

All primary learned models use a $256$-wide, $3$-layer tanh MLP, Adam, and a cosine-annealed
learning rate, and train on a single 8\,GB consumer GPU. Mixed-$k$ rollout adds negligible
parameters; its cost is wall-clock time (the HNN mixed-window run is the most expensive at
${\sim}6.7$\,h because each unroll step recomputes $\nabla H$ via autograd). Chronos-2 runs at
inference only. The
dataset has $67{,}491$ samples across $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ and three regimes, subsampled at
$\Delta t{=}0.01$\,s from the $10^{-3}$\,s ground truth. Despite the sample count, the
training set contains only \emph{nine} trajectories, one for each $(k,\mathrm{regime})$ cell. The
budget study uses nested subsets of 1, 5, and 20 trajectories per eligible cell and three
training initializations. Neural~ODE therefore sees $9$, $45$, or $180$ trajectories; HNN sees $3$,
$15$, or $60$ undamped trajectories. Evaluation uses the same two held-out seeds throughout.

\paragraph{Training objectives.} Derivative matching minimizes MSE on finite-difference
$\dot{\mathbf{s}}$; rollout loss minimizes MSE on the state after $w$ Euler steps with
gradients through the unroll; mixed-window rollout trains on several window lengths jointly
(Neural~ODE: $w\in\{10,50\}$; HNN: $w\in\{5,20\}$). The LNN's loss requires second-order
automatic differentiation; the LNN trains at full scale ($67$k samples, hidden 256) on a CUDA GPU.
Chronos-2 resamples the context to at most $512$ points and forecasts at most $64$ steps.

\section{Related work}

The audited models span learned dynamics \citep{chen2018node,greydanus2019hnn,cranmer2020lnn}

failed cells here stay in the main score because models are compared at fixed task coverage.

\section{Real-data check details}
\label{app:realdata}
The hardware dataset \citep{myers2020pendulum} is openly released via Mendeley Data
(CC~BY~4.0 listing; the deposit includes a GPLv3 license file) with video tracking of
both link angles at $500$\,Hz and per-frame uncertainty estimates (median $0.20^{\circ}$ upper
link, $0.43^{\circ}$ lower). We use the published simplified point-mass parameters
($L=[0.172,0.143]$\,m, $m=[0.311,0.111]$\,kg, $g=9.81$\,m/s$^2$). Angular velocities come from a
Savitzky--Golay filter (window $42$\,ms, cubic, analytic derivative). The recordings begin
with the pendulum positioned by hand, so the free drop is detected as the first time either
link exceeds $4$\,rad/s; evaluation states are drawn evenly from $0.2$ to $24$\,s after the
drop, $60$ per trial. The tracker zeroes its angle at the first frame, leaving a
gravity-referenced offset per link; every trial ends hanging at rest with tracked angles at
exact multiples of $360^{\circ}$ (within $0.08^{\circ}$), which fixes the offset at zero
independently of any model. A $20$\,ms physics-tracking check agrees, with error of
$0.005$--$0.007$\,rad against a $0.006$\,rad tracking floor. A grid search over
$\{0,5{\times}10^{-4},\dots,2{\times}10^{-2}\}$ selects damping $10^{-3}$ on Trial~1 at the $0.5$\,s
horizon. SINDy uses a degree-two polynomial plus first-order Fourier library on Trial~1 with
Savitzky--Golay derivatives; Chronos-2 receives a $5$\,s context resampled to $50$\,Hz and
forecasts at most $50$ steps. Failures are scored at $\pi/2$; Chronos-2's are input-infeasible cells (insufficient
context) rather than divergences, and the fixed-coverage rule scores both alike. The tracking
dataset has been public since 2020 and may appear in foundation-model corpora; contamination
would inflate Chronos-2's score, so its baseline-level result is, if anything, conservative. Paired
bootstrap differences over the $120$ states at $1.0$\,s: RK4 minus persistence $+0.018$
($95\%$ CI $[-0.121,+0.155]$); damped minus undamped RK4 $-0.103$ ($[-0.202,-0.003]$), so
the damping gain at the longest horizon is significant while the RK4--persistence gap is
not. States sampled along a trajectory are temporally dependent, so we repeat both with a
moving-block bootstrap within each trial (blocks of $10$ consecutive states, ${\sim}4$\,s):
the conclusions are unchanged (damped minus RK4 $[-0.179,-0.019]$; RK4 minus persistence
$[-0.131,+0.064]$), and remain so for block lengths from $2$ to $12$\,s.

\section{Extended diagnostics}
\label{app:diagnostics}
This appendix expands the diagnostics summarized in \S\ref{sec:behavior}.

\paragraph{Behavior by horizon.}
Out-of-sample error by horizon appears in Table~\ref{tab:horizon}. At $1$\,s the ordering
follows the main comparison: the integrators are near-exact, \texttt{kimi} ($0.457$) has the
lowest black-box error, and the learned models are at $0.86$--$1.25$. At $10$\,s all models
are in the chaotic regime, and the ordering is stable: \texttt{kimi} ($0.619$) remains lowest
among black-box methods while the learned models reach $1.09$--$1.46$. The mixed-window
Neural~ODE is intermediate at both horizons ($0.951$/$1.120$).
Figure~\ref{fig:errorgrowth} shows the growth curves over the
$\{0.01,1,10,60\}$\,s ladder: the numerical integrators stay near zero until ${\sim}10$\,s,
whereas every black-box family saturates near the chaotic noise floor by $1$\,s. The
$\{1,10\}$\,s window used for the comparison is the range over which the families remain
separable.

\paragraph{Breakdown by $k$ and regime.}
Tables~\ref{tab:perk} and~\ref{tab:perregime} report out-of-sample error by
number of links and by regime. Error increases with $k$ for every model except the mixed-window
Neural~ODE, which dips at $k{=}3$ (for \texttt{kimi}, $0.11\to0.55\to0.95$; the integrators
are near-exact at $k{=}1$ and degrade as chaos sets in), and the cross-family ordering holds
within each $k$. The
\texttt{changed\_hidden} regime has the lowest error for most models (for
\texttt{kimi}, $0.46$ hidden vs.\ $0.64$ normal). For the learned models, withholding the
constants reduces but does not remove the difficulty effect: hidden-regime errors
are now $0.71$--$1.19$; the earlier values favor models given the true constants. The
low-gravity hidden constants ($g{=}1.62$) produce slower, less-divergent motion over a fixed
horizon, so the unadjusted hidden-regime advantage is a difficulty confound
(\S\ref{sec:sysid}). A cluster bootstrap over complete held-out trajectories changes no
confidence interval by more than ${\sim}0.03$\,rad and leaves the ranking unchanged.

\paragraph{Training-objective check.}
Table~\ref{tab:objective} reports the effect of the loss with architecture fixed. Under the
equalized hidden-regime run, rollout loss is not uniformly better: mixed-window rollout
is lower for the Neural~ODE, while single-$k$ rollout is lower for the HNN. Full-scale CUDA
training of the LNN gives the highest learned-model error ($1.356$), with $6\%$ of cells
diverging or exceeding the rollout timeout after memory-safe recovery. We make no
architecture-wide claim about LNNs; under our implementation and protocol the LNN is
numerically unstable and generalizes poorly out of sample.

\paragraph{Physical-fidelity check.}
Hamiltonian and Lagrangian networks target structure preservation, so we evaluate energy
drift on the undamped (\texttt{normal}) regime, where true energy is constant. Table~\ref{tab:energy}
reports mean absolute drift over $10$\,s, normalized by $E^{*}{=}g\sum_i m_i L_i$, and the
fraction of bounded rollouts. The learned models do not match numerical integrators on this
axis: the lowest-drift learned model is $0.779$, compared with $0.066$ for the symplectic
integrator and $0.494$ for Euler. Symplectic rollout of the HNN leaves drift nearly unchanged,
so changing the integrator does not account for the HNN's drift. At the derivative-matching
objective, HNN drift is comparable to Neural~ODE drift ($4.27$ vs.\ $4.35$); the LNN is
unstable, with $27\%$ bounded rollouts.
These results use the same nine-trajectory learned-model budget as the main comparison.

\paragraph{Multivariate time-series check.}
On $720$ paired samples, Chronos-2 multivariate and univariate errors are indistinguishable:
$0.543$ (95\% CI $[0.498,0.589]$) versus $0.549$ ($[0.505,0.593]$). No effect remains after
Holm correction, so the larger study rejects the apparent per-$k$ trend in the primary grid.

\paragraph{Predictability horizon.}
Pointwise error past the Lyapunov time saturates to a system-dependent constant for every
model, so long-horizon error rankings carry little information. We define the predictability
horizon as the longest horizon a model tracks contiguously below a threshold $\tau$ from
$t{=}0$; the contiguous rule is used because, after divergence, a later horizon can fall below
$\tau$ by angle-wrap coincidence. Table~\ref{tab:predhorizon} sweeps $\tau$.
