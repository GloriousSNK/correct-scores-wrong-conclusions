## 1. Purpose

The original full-matrix design measures pointwise coordinate error across horizons up
to 60s. Because the double/triple pendulum is chaotic (positive Lyapunov exponent),
pointwise prediction is impossible past ~1 Lyapunov time, so those long-horizon cells
saturate to the noise floor for every model and carry no signal. (See the first-pass
results and `RESEARCH_IDEA_EVALUATION.md`.)

This mini-pass **reframes** the experiment around the two questions that actually have
signal:

1. **Predictability cliff** — *where* does each model's error depart from the
   ground-truth integrator? Sampled densely over short horizons
   `[0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0] s`.
2. **In-context system identification** — does hiding the physical constants
   (`changed_hidden`: model must infer g, L, m) degrade predictions vs.
   `changed_disclosed` (constants given)?

**Scope:** 3 systems (k=1,2,3) × 3 regimes × 3 trajectories × 7 horizons, coords
modality, `no_cot`. Models: 3 deployed LLMs + 3 numerical baselines.
`gpt-5.5` is **not** included — the subscription has 0 TPM quota for it; a quota
increase has been requested and it will be added once granted.

## 3. Predictability cliff — mean coordinate error by horizon

Lower is better. Successful cells only.

| Horizon | grok | llama | kimi¹ | rk4 | euler |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01s | 0.0002 | 0.0012 | 0.0001 | 0.0000 | 0.0004 |
| 0.05s | 0.0034 | 0.206 | 0.0006 | 0.0000 | 0.0021 |
| 0.1s | 0.0078 | 0.275 | 0.017 | 0.0000 | 0.0045 |
| 0.25s | 0.041 | 0.431 | 0.031 | 0.0000 | 0.013 |
| 0.5s | **0.433** | 0.808 | 0.258 | 0.0000 | 0.025 |
| 1.0s | 0.723 | 1.074 | 0.824 | 0.0000 | 0.083 |
| 2.0s | 1.073 | 0.980 | 0.803 | 0.0000 | 0.291 |

¹ kimi values are from a biased 18% sample (mostly single-pendulum); not directly
comparable. See §5.

**Findings**

- **grok has a locatable predictability cliff at ~0.25–0.5s.** It tracks the dynamics
  well to 0.25s (0.04), then falls off sharply. This is exactly the metric the reframe
  was built to expose — the dense sampling makes the break point visible.
- **Crude Euler beats every LLM at every horizon past 0.01s.** At 0.5s, Euler (0.025)
  is ~17× better than grok and ~32× better than llama. The LLMs mimic a single
  integration step but do not perform competitive multi-step integration.
- **llama-4-maverick is weak from the start** — error 0.21 already at 0.05s. The
  non-reasoning model is poor at precise numeric extrapolation even short-term.
- **rk4 is exact** (it is the data-generating integrator), so it wins by construction;
  it serves as the oracle, not a competitor.

## 4. Hidden-constants probe (system identification)

Restricted to horizons ≤ 0.25s, where prediction is actually possible. Mean coord error.

| Model | Disclosed (told g,L,m) | Hidden (must infer) | Hidden / Disclosed | n (D / H) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| grok-4-1-fast-reasoning | 0.0047 | 0.0247 | **5.2× worse** | 23 / 33 |
| llama-4-maverick | 0.336 | 0.019 | 0.06 | 32 / 31 |
| kimi-k2.6 | 0.0000 | 0.012 | — | **2** / 11 |

Normal-regime baseline (≤0.25s): grok 0.0030 (n=20), llama 0.310 (n=31), kimi 0.0000 (n=6).

**Findings**

- **grok is the only clean signal: 5× worse when constants are hidden.** It genuinely
  *uses* the disclosed g/L/m and **cannot reliably infer them** from a short trajectory —
  a concrete (if preliminary) answer to the system-identification question.
- **llama is not interpretable here** — its error is large in every regime (~0.31 even
  in `normal`), so "hidden looks better" is noise, not inference skill.
- **kimi disclosed n=2** — statistically meaningless; ignore the ratio.

**Caveat:** these are mini-pass sample sizes (3 trajectories/cell). Directionally
informative, not publication-grade. A dedicated hidden-constants pass with more
trajectories is the recommended next step.

## 5. kimi-k2.6 — why it could not handle the task

**kimi-k2.6 was retained in the benchmark, but it largely fails by design.** This is a
real result about the model, not an infrastructure defect. Details:

- **It is a "thinking" model.** Kimi-K2.6 (Moonshot) emits extended chain-of-thought in
  a separate `reasoning_content` field and leaves the `content` (answer) field empty
  until its reasoning completes. There is no way to disable this; it reasons even in the
  `no_cot` condition.
- **It reasons enormously for this task** — measured **7,600–16,000+ output tokens per
  single prediction**, vs. ~30–60 tokens for grok/llama.
- **Single-pendulum (k=1) usually fits** within the 16,384-token cap → those cells
  passed (this is the easy-biased 18% that succeeded).
- **Double/triple pendulum (k=2, k=3) reason past 16,384 tokens** and hit the cap
  (`finish_reason = length`) **without ever emitting the final JSON answer** → parse
  failure. Raising the cap to 16,384 (from 256→2048→16,384) recovered k=1 but not the
  multi-pendulum cells.
- **Each failed call still burns the full 16,384 output tokens**, so failures are not
  free.

**Consequences for the benchmark**

- 18% success rate, heavily biased toward k=1 → kimi's accuracy numbers are **not
  comparable** to the other models and should be read with this caveat everywhere.
- **Cost:** kimi consumed **2.9M output tokens** in this mini-pass and accounted for
  **~99% of the total spend** (see §6) — ~50× the cost of grok/llama for far worse
  coverage.

**Options to make kimi viable (not yet applied):** raise the token cap to 32k–64k (lets
k=2 finish, multiplies cost/latency, may still cap on k=3), or run kimi k=1-only. As of
this pass, kimi is documented as **impractical at the scale/cost of a full sweep**.

## 7. Methodology / infrastructure notes (reproducibility)

Getting the live Azure path working required several fixes, documented here for
reproducibility:

1. **Endpoint:** the resource's legacy `/models` inference route returns 404. It serves
   the **OpenAI-compatible `/openai/v1/`** route. The predictor (`bench/models/azure_llm.py`)
   was rewritten from `azure-ai-inference` to the **`openai` AsyncOpenAI** SDK, with the
   base URL normalized to `…/openai/v1`. Added `openai` + `aiohttp` to `requirements.txt`.
2. **Timeouts:** reasoning models are slow (grok ~37–92s/call). `request_timeout` raised
   to 300s and `max_retries` lowered to 2 to bound per-cell waste.
3. **Token budget:** raised `max_tokens` to 16,384 (from 256) and added a
   `reasoning_content` fallback so reasoning models can finish and emit JSON. (kimi still
   exceeds this on multi-pendulum — see §5.)
4. **Budgets:** 7 cost budgets ($1 / $10 / $50 / $100 / $200 / $500 / $10,000) created on
   the `eval-benchmark` RG with 80%/100% actual + forecast email alerts.

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Predictability cliff is the right headline metric** — report where each model
   departs from the oracle (grok ≈ 0.25–0.5s), not 60s pointwise error.
2. **The LLM-vs-Euler gap is the honest story** — frontier LLMs mimic one integration
   step but are beaten by crude Euler at every horizon beyond it.
3. **grok shows real (preliminary) reliance on disclosed constants** (5× degradation
   when hidden) → worth a dedicated, larger hidden-constants pass.
4. **kimi-k2.6:** retained but flagged impractical (cost/coverage). Decide per-model
   token policy or restrict to k=1 before any full sweep.
5. **Add gpt-5.5** once the quota request is approved (vision-capable; also unlocks the
   image-modality experiments).
